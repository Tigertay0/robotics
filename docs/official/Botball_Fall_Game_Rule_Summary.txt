Virtual Fall Game
Teams earn points by participating in the seasonal game rules—such as sorting and moving 
designated objects and Botguy to different areas.
Virtual Tournaments requirements

Participating teams must have a competition game board that meets the requirements 
specified in the table build document.

Access to the internet 

Two cameras for competition day: one static overview camera showing the entire game 
board, and one movable camera for judge's robot inspection and point verification.

Minor differences related to game board setup may be accommodated by the Head Judge at 
his or her discretion.

An adult must be present for the entire tournament and will attest to the specification of the 
game board, robots, and game pieces being used. 
Game Board Areas
The board this year uses five black-tape-delineated zones, plus a Raised Platform, the area 
beneath it, and a separate Bottle/Ring enclosure.

Zone 1: Top-left zone.

Zone 2: Top-right zone.

Zone 3: Middle-left zone (pom side).

Zone 4: Middle-right zone (duck side).

Zone 5: Lower-left zone — doubles as the larger Starting Box.
Excluded from the Overall Multiplier count (see Scoring Rules): the Starting Box, the Raised 
Platform, the area beneath the Raised Platform, and the Bottle/Ring enclosure (a distinct area 
bordering, but not part of, zone 5).
The starting box is 11.25" × 21.125" with a virtual height limit of 12".

Scoring Rules
Foundational Rules

Black Tape Rule: Any game piece touching black tape does not score, except cups (cups 
are exempt from this rule only for their Nesting and Flat/Upright bonuses — a cup touching 
tape still fails to earn Base Score).

General Scoring Rule: A game piece must touch the surface of the scoring area to score, 
except pieces following the Volume Rule.

Volume Rule (Ring/Bottle only): To score a ring on a bottle at the 15-point level, the 
bottle neck must break the interior volume of the ring.

Highest Scoring Rule: A game piece can only score in one scoring area and will be scored 
as if it is in the highest scoring area, as determined by base score without multipliers.

Robot Rule: A robot is defined minimally as a KIPR Robot Controller with at least two 
motors connected to it that leaves the Starting Box. A robot with 2 controllers counts as a 
single robot.

Final Scoring Rule: The score is determined by final object location, not by how it got 
there. Judges will wait until any scoring objects still in motion have come to rest before 
scoring a game.
Base Score
Every scoring piece touching the board surface, off black tape, scores 1 point. This applies in 
addition to any special scoring described below — Base Score and special scoring are additive, 
not exclusive.
Pom Scoring
For each group of poms found together in a single zone, apply the following:

Same color, sorted, 1–4 poms: linear — 1, 2, 3, or 4 points.

Same color, sorted, all 5 poms of that color together (a Complete Set) and no other colors 
present: flat 25-point bonus. There are exactly 5 poms of each color (4 visible + 1 hidden), 
so no duplicate-color ambiguity is possible.

--- page 6 ---

All different colors present, no duplicates, n poms: n² points (e.g., 2 poms/2 colors = 4, 3/3 
= 9, 4/4 = 16, 5/5 = 25).

Mixed group (some duplicate colors, some unique, neither fully sorted nor fully unique): falls 
back to Base Score only — 1 point per pom, no additional bonus.
Mystery Red Pom
One of the 5 hidden poms is red. If that red pom, and only that pom, ends up isolated on top of 
the Raised Platform (no other poms with it), it scores a flat 50-point bonus. This bonus is 
multiplied by the Overall Multiplier (see below). Any other pom present with it voids the bonus 
— this discourages simply dumping all poms onto the platform.
Duck Scoring

Base Score: 1 point per duck, off tape (as with any piece).

Under the Raised Platform: 3 points per duck.

Ducks do not break sorts and have no color-sorting bonus of their own.
Overall Multiplier
Each of the 5 numbered zones that contains exactly 1 duck (no more, no fewer) adds +1 to a 
single, game-wide multiplier. This multiplier applies to the entire round's score — the full Round 
Subtotal, including the Mystery Red Pom bonus.

0 or 1 qualifying zone: multiplier is 1x (no effect).

2 qualifying zones: 2x. 3: 3x. And so on, up to a maximum of 5x.
The smaller Starting Box, the Raised Platform, the area beneath the Raised Platform, and the 
Bottle/Ring enclosure do not count toward this multiplier.
Cup Scoring

Touching black tape, no special condition: 0 points.

Off tape, on board, no special condition: 1 point (Base Score).

Nested (one cup inside the other's volume), regardless of tape: 20 points.

Flat/upright on the board (per cup, regardless of nesting or tape): 20 points each.

Both cups nested and both flat/upright at once: 20 + 20 + 20 = 60 points.
Cups are the one piece exempt from the Black Tape Rule, but only for the Nesting and 
Flat/Upright bonuses above — a cup on tape still earns 0 toward Base Score.
Ring & Bottle Scoring

Ring touching the bottle, not on the neck: 3 points.

Ring on the bottle neck (bottle neck breaks the ring's interior volume): 15 points.
Pickleball Scoring

Under the Raised Platform: 7 points each.
Botguy & Big Duck

Botguy on top of the Raised Platform: 15 points.

Big Duck at Botguy's starting position: 15 points.

Definitions
Board & Zones
Unless otherwise noted, all zones are delineated by the inner edge of the black tape and the 
vertical projection of any PVC borders.

Black Tape: The boundary marking that, when touched by a scoring piece (except cups' 
Nesting/Flat bonuses), disqualifies that piece from scoring.

Starting Box: The two designated boxes (one larger, one smaller) robots must start within; 
robots must individually fit within the smaller box, though one may start in the larger.

Multiplier Zone: One of the 5 numbered zones that, when containing exactly one duck, 
adds +1 to the Overall Multiplier.

Raised Platform: The elevated scoring area where Botguy and the Mystery Red Pom can 
score. Delineated by the edges of the corrugated plastic.

Area Under the Raised Platform: A separate scoring zone beneath the platform, where 
pickleballs and ducks score.

Bottle/Ring Enclosure: The post area holding rings and blue bottles; distinct from, and 
bordering, but not part of, zone 5.

Pieces

Botguy: The robot supervisor piece, starting at the top-center, far edge of the table.

Big Duck: The distinct, larger duck piece, starting atop the Raised Platform.

Pickleball: Scoring piece (4 total), mounted on posts.

Pom: Small scoring piece; 5 colors of 5 each (4 visible + 1 hidden).

Mystery Pom: The specific hidden red pom, starting beneath a cup.

Cup: Scoring piece (green, teal); can nest inside another cup and/or stand flat/upright.

Blue Bottle: Scoring piece; rings score by breaking its neck's interior volume.

Ring: Scoring piece (6 total, 3 per post); scores by touching or capturing a bottle neck.

Rubber Duck: 5 small colored ducks; contribute to multiplier zones and under-platform 
scoring.
Scoring Mechanics

Base Score: The flat 1-point award for any piece touching the board surface, off black tape.

Nesting: One cup placed inside another cup's volume.

Flat/Upright: A cup resting with its bottom flat against the board surface.

Complete Set: All 5 poms of a single color together, alone, in one zone.

Breaking the Volume: When one piece's relevant feature passes into another piece's open 
interior (used directionally for rings/bottles).
Tie Breakers & Special Scoring Conditions
Two disqualifying conditions apply, in this order of priority:
1. A robot must leave the Starting Box during the round; a robot that never breaks the Starting 
Box boundary (including the 12" ceiling) loses / scores 0 for the round.
2. A robot must stop driving its motors and stop servo motion by the end of the round; failure 
to do so results in automatic disqualification for that round (see End of Game).
Seeding Rounds
Seeding is not head-to-head. Each team earns an individual score; there is no winner, loser, or 
tie.
Double Elimination Rounds (Non-Finals)
If two teams' scores are tied:
1. Replay the match once.
2. If still tied, work through this ordered tiebreaker list:

Most rings on bottle necks

Most complete pom sets

Most ducks counted toward the Overall Multiplier

Most cups flipped (flat/upright)

Most pickleballs moved (scored under the platform)

Scoring the red Mystery Pom

Closest to Botguy (measured from the nearest corner of the robot's Wombat controller to 
Botguy)
All tiebreakers are measured entirely within each team's own single-sided board — none 
depend on an opponent's robot or pieces being present.
Double Elimination Finals
Tiebreakers are not used in the Finals. The match will be replayed until one team scores strictly 
more points than the other.

Gameplay
Fair Play and Spirit of Botball
Botball is about the development of student skills. All adults supervising a team should 
understand that responsible Botball mentorship does not include working on the robot entries 
or programming the robot entries for the students but does allow for appropriate mentor 
guidance of the team. Virtual Events require an adult to be present for the entire tournament 
and who will attest to the specifications of the game board and game pieces being used.
Spirit of Botball: This is a 100% student-driven experience. Students know this, 
and adults know better!
Inspections
A virtual judge may ask for a part count or to view robots in a zoomed-in view on the camera. 
The mentoring adult/coach will help the judge with the inspection and attest to the compliance 
of the robot.
Setup — Before Hands-Off

Up to two students from a team may bring the team's robot(s) to the tournament table and 
perform setup.

Teams will have 90 seconds to complete setup and calibration, placing robot(s) within their 
Starting Box(es) as desired.

Starting light rules: lights are positioned on the outside edge of the game table; teams must 
adjust sensor mounts to match; lights must be aimed at the team's sensors or the floor; 
lights may not break the vertical projection of the board inside its PVC boundary; two 
connected starting lights per team, one per controller; teams cannot touch starting lights or 
any part of the table after Hands-Off.

Teams must notify table judges before the end of "Hands-Off" if they believe the table is not 
set up properly or if there is something else they need to adjust. The team will receive 
confirmation from the judge, make the adjustment, and then — Hands-Off!

If judges determine a team is taking too long to calibrate, they will issue a warning and set a 
60-second timer. A team not ready after 60 seconds may be disqualified from the round. 
Maximum setup time (extendable at judges' discretion) is 90 seconds.
Timeout
Each team is granted a single timeout for the entire tournament, functioning the same as an in-
person timeout, but without a physical card — teams notify the judge when they wish to use it. 
Timeouts may only be requested while the team is at the table before "Hands-Off." Once 
granted, the team receives a 3-minute timeout, which may be used to make adjustments to 
their robot, but not to practice runs at the table — however, teams may practice the starting 
light sequence during this time. Teams are advised to save their timeout for the Double 
Elimination rounds, as Seeding rounds are the best 2 out of 3.
If a robot is deemed to have an illegal part during Seeding rounds, a team may use their 
timeout to remove the offending part. If a robot is deemed to have an illegal part during Double 
Elimination rounds, the robot will be disqualified.
If any parts are added or modified during the timeout period, the Head Judge may ask to have 
the robot re-inspected once the timeout ends.
Before the Game Begins — After Hands-Off
Once "Hands-Off" has been declared, team members will position themselves so as not to block 
the judge's or the overview camera's view of the game table. No part of a team's robot(s) may 
leave the Starting Box(es) until the round has begun. Movement is okay so long as the Starting 

--- page 10 ---
Box(es) boundary isn't violated. If a moving violation happens, the judges will call a fault on the 
team. Team members may not move the starting lights at any time after Hands-Off. Team 
members may not signal to their robots after "Hands-Off" to start their robots. If a team 
member at the table is wearing a shirt with large solid colors similar to game pieces on the 
table, they will be asked to crouch or reposition during the round so as not to interfere with the 
robot's camera.
Teams are responsible for controlling outside light sources at their home table to prevent 
accidental triggering of the light sensors before the round starts. This may include shielding 
light sensors from windows, room lighting, or other environmental sources as needed to ensure 
a reliable start.
After the Game Begins — Lights On
Once the starting lights have turned on, the round counts unless a judge rules otherwise. At the 
start of the game, the starting lights turn on and robots are then allowed to leave the Starting 
Box.
The round lasts two minutes (120 seconds). The lighting sequence is:

0 seconds: lights turn on; robots can leave starting boxes

5 seconds: lights turn off

120 seconds: game over; robots must turn off motors and freeze/power down servos
Because this is a virtual tournament, teams may use a simpler home light-triggering setup and 
are not required to have an end-of-round light warning (e.g., a blink sequence). The 120-second 
time limit is enforced regardless of whether a team's home lighting reflects the end of the 
round — teams are responsible for tracking the round timer independently to ensure their robot 
stops on time.
End of Game
Robots must stop driving their motors and stop servo motion by the end of the round. Failure to 
do so results in automatic disqualification for that round. Incidental motion from a servo holding 
a position under load is OK.
If a team never breaks the boundary of their Starting Box during the round, that results in a 
score of 0 for the round (Seeding) — see Tie Breakers & Special Scoring Conditions for the 
equivalent Double Elimination handling.
Scoring is based on the location of pieces at the end of the round, not how the pieces got there. 
Scoring takes place once the round has ended and all items have come to rest.
If all motion has stopped before 120 seconds, the judge may ask the team if their robot is done. 
If the team agrees, the round may be ended early.
Final Scoring and Rulings
If a team does not agree with the score as calculated, they must immediately notify the judge 
before leaving the table and before any items have been moved. If they do not agree with the 
judge's ruling, they may ask to speak with the Head Judge. The Head Judge will spend no more 
than 5 minutes on the decision. Teams must initial the score sheet before leaving the table, 
signifying acceptance of the ruling. If they do not agree with the ruling, the Head Judge can sign 
for the team to progress the event forward.
There are no instant replays. No external videos will be used for replay scoring. If a team 
disagrees with a judge's decision, only the two team members at the table may politely discuss 
the issue with the table judge and/or Head Judge. Issues with the scoring after the teams and 
judges have agreed may not be considered.

The Head Judge reserves the right to make rulings on specific rules or wording in the game 
review that will be in effect for the remainder of the tournament.
Spirit of Botball: Mentors, spectators, and team members should respect teams' 
and judges' final decisions. A Head Judge may overrule any previous rule or 
loophole that they believe to violate the Spirit of Botball.
Acknowledgements
The KIPR Robot Controller is a powerful device, but the use of threading can cause 
unpredictable results, such as the robot not stopping when utilizing the shutdown in function. 
Teams are encouraged to limit their use of threading and to make sure they take precautionary 
steps to stop their robot within the time limit of the game.
Tournament Structure
Seeding Rounds
There will be three Seeding rounds, single-sided. A team's Seed Score is the average of their 
best two of three runs. Seed scores of less than 0 are counted as 0.
Double Elimination (DE) Rounds
A team is out of the Double Elimination tournament when it has lost two games. Teams 
compete virtually and simultaneously on their own single-sided boards; scores are compared to 
determine the winner of each match.
Not part of this game: Documentation scoring, Double Seeding, Alliance Matches, and Tiered 
Double Elimination rounds.
Virtual Tournaments
Virtual Tournaments require the team to have a competition game board that meets 
requirements, access to the internet, and two cameras: one static overview camera showing 
the whole game board, and one moveable camera for robot inspection and point verification on 
each run. Some exceptions to rules related to game board setup may be accommodated by the 
Head Judge.
Construction Rules
The official construction rules for the 2026 Botball Fall Game consist of the latest revision of this 
2026 Botball Fall Game Review document and any updated game rules posted on the FAQ.
Kit Rules

Sensors from the 2017, 2018, 2019, and 2020-2026 kits may be used so long as they don't 
exceed the type or number in the 2026 kit.

Teams may use two cameras.

KIPR Metal Parts — Aluminum chassis (old KIPR chassis) is not allowed.

Robots may be constructed out of any or all of this year's kit parts except: boxes, bags, 
wrapping or packing material, chargers, download cables, wrenches, screwdriver, and color 
stickers. The included camera is the only USB device that may be plugged into a robot 
during the game.

Small removable mounting dots/strips (Glue Dots, UGlu, Scotch Restickable Dots/Strips, blue 
tack) may be used for construction. Hot melt glue or other adhesives are not allowed.

Zip ties used solely for wire management may be used as needed, without limit. Zip ties 
used for any other purpose (structural, mounting, etc.) are limited to five (5) total. Teams 

--- page 12 ---
are limited to the standard 4" white zip ties included in the kit for both purposes; damaged 
ties may be replaced with ones of equivalent size (black or white).

Up to 100cm of thread, string, or fishing line (max diameter 1mm, non-metallic only) may be 
used, except for offensive measures such as entanglement.

Paper (max 20#), foam board, or corrugated plastic, up to one standard letter/A4 sheet 
each, may be added; must be black or white/grayscale only.

Up to 10 standard office rubber bands (max size #19) may be used; may not be glued or 
melted.

Up to 10 metal paper clips (1"–1.5") may be used; cannot be cut, broken, or plugged into 
wiring.

Coins, up to 250 grams, may be used as a counterweight only.

The light sensors in the kit do not require a light guide unless there is significant direct 
sunlight in the room.

Lego parts cannot be physically modified, except threading axle holes with screws.

Metal parts may not be cut or broken to a smaller size; only straps and plates may be bent. 
Brackets cannot be bent.

Teams are limited to using only 4' of the ABS plastic rods they receive.
3D Print Rules

Allowed filaments: PLA or PETG.

All printed parts must be grayscale-colored, regardless of material.

Parts are measured by their total exterior bounding box volume, calculated in whole-inch 
units only — each dimension (height, length, width) is rounded up to the next whole inch 
before multiplying.

Small parts #6 (60mm × 60mm × 20mm)

Mid-size parts #2 (220mm × 220mm × 80mm).

Large parts #1 (220mm × 220mm × 250mm).

Anti-circumvention clause: Splitting, segmenting, or otherwise designing parts with the 
intent of circumventing these volume-based limits — including assemblies of smaller prints 
that combine (by tethering, adjacency, or assembly) into a functionally or physically 
monolithic part — will be evaluated by the Head Judge as a single combined part for rule 
purposes. Judges retain full discretion to disqualify or require modification of any print 
determined to violate the intent of this rule.

A single part cannot exceed the print volume of an Ender 3 V3 SE (220mm × 220mm × 
250mm).

An STL file must be submitted for each part in use prior to the tournament, per the schedule 
posted on the Team Homebase.
Robot Logistics

Each robot, if named, can only have a name (G-rated) approved by an adult team leader 
before the tournament.

Multiple processors (such as two KIPR robot controllers) may exist on a single robot.

You may only use the Wombat controllers.

It is not necessary to use all the parts in a kit.

Interim rule: every robot must fit within the footprint of the smaller Starting Box, 11.25" × 
21.125" × 12".

All elements of a team's entry must be within the volume of a Starting Box at game start.

After the game starts, robots are allowed to expand in size.

Starting light sensors may be shielded as needed; neither the sensor nor shielding may 
extend outside the Starting Box.

All independent structures not under computer control should be clearly marked with the 
team's number.

Robot teams can have a maximum of 4 independent structures on the game table at a time.


The ABS Plastic Rod must be permanently affixed to a robot by at least one end and may 
not be used as a projectile or independent structure.

No electrical modifications may be made to any KIPR robot controller, sensors, or motors, 
except battery substitution with one approved by KIPR.

No wire extensions may be used except those provided in the kit.

Offensive entanglement strategies are not in line with the Spirit of Botball and may be 
subject to disqualification.
Safety

No untethered robot-launched projectiles, other than game pieces, are allowed.

No tethered projectiles containing metal pieces are allowed.

No metal pieces or wires are to be used in effectors that move or rotate at high speed.

No metal protrusions are to be used that are likely to cause electrical or safety risks for 
other robots (including arms and projectiles).

Judges will determine how safe a robot is.

Electrical tape (black or white) may be used to cover metal pieces deemed a safety risk. 
Tape may not be used for structural purposes.

If the Head Judge decides a robot is not considered safe, it will not be allowed to run until 
modified or will be removed from the table.
External Communication

No external communications (e.g., IR, Bluetooth, wireless, or semaphores) may be used 
during tournament play, with the exception of robot to robot (for a team's own multiple 
robots).

Teams found programming a robot while at the table or in the on-deck area during rounds, 
excluding during a timeout, will receive a DQ for the round.

USB cables and chargers may not be used during game table tournament play.

A team's robot controller may have WiFi turned on or off at the tournament.
